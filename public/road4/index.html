How it happened

You became the whole business.

You were good at the work, so you did the work. Then the selling. Then the invoices, the hiring, the late fixes, the posts at eleven at night. Nobody else was going to. And now the business runs on your hours, your memory and your mood.

  • Your hours are the ceiling. The business cannot grow past the edge of your week.
  • Your biggest decisions get made tired, late at night, or not at all.
  • Your people stop offering, because you’ll redo it anyway.
  • You quote low, because there was never time to work out what an hour of you is worth.
  • You can’t remember the last week you spent on the work you started this for.

Doing everything yourself feels like commitment. It is the ceiling.

Two kinds of stuck

Which one were you this week?

A business that grows needs three things at once: a reason you can say in one sentence, a strategy that says where and how, and the effort to do the work that matters. Founders get stuck by having one without the others.

    Be honest with yourself

    Is this for you?

    It is, if…

    • everything still comes back to you;
    • you are building, growing or scaling something real, with customers or close to them;
    • you will look at your own calendar, customers and numbers, even when you’d rather not;
    • you would rather see the truth than hear a pep talk.

    It isn’t, if…

    • you want a plan to admire rather than one you run;
    • you want a tactic that works without you changing anything;
    • you want a course to watch. This is work you do, in your own business, with your own evidence.
    I don’t have time for this.

    That is usually the symptom, not the reason. Week one starts by counting where your hours actually went, so the first thing it gives you is time you can see. Each week ends with one real thing done by Sunday, at your pace.

    Is this personal development or business?

    Mostly business. The first of the four parts is you, because in a small business the founder is the first system. The other three are your people, how the work runs, and your market: customers, price and pipeline.

    I’ve done courses before. Why would this be different?

    A course gives you ideas. Every step here sends you to something real, your calendar, your customers, your messages, and ends in a decision with a date. After week three, a person reads what you wrote.

    I work alone. Is “leading people” for me?

    Yes. Your people are already there: the contractor, the partner, the client, the family member who covers for you. And the first person you lead is yourself, on a week when nobody is checking.
