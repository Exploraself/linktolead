## c02 · One list saved the plane

- On 30 October 1935 the Boeing Model 299 crashed during an evaluation flight at Wright Field, Dayton, Ohio.  
  *Check:* Atul Gawande, The Checklist Manifesto (2009), chapter 2; National Museum of the US Air Force, “Boeing Model 299” fact sheet.
- The cause was a control gust lock left engaged before take-off.  
  *Check:* Same sources.
- A newspaper called it “too much airplane for one man to fly” (paraphrased on the slide).  
  *Check:* Gawande, The Checklist Manifesto, chapter 2.
- Test pilots then created a pilot’s checklist; the Army Air Corps went on to order the plane, which became the B-17.  
  *Check:* Gawande, chapter 2; National Museum of the US Air Force.
- Explaining a task again every week: 30 minutes × 52 weeks = 26 hours a year.  
  *Check:* Arithmetic, illustrative numbers.

## c03 · 10% off = 50% more work

- Example: price $1,000, costs $700, left $300 (30% margin). With 10% off: $900 − $700 = $200 left, a third less.  
  *Check:* Arithmetic, illustrative numbers.
- To keep $3,000: 10 sales at $300, or 15 sales at $200 (50% more).  
  *Check:* Arithmetic: 3,000 ÷ 300 = 10; 3,000 ÷ 200 = 15.
- Sales needed for $3,000 at a 30% margin: no discount 10; 5% off 12; 10% off 15; 15% off 20; 20% off 30.  
  *Check:* Arithmetic: 3,000 ÷ (300, 250, 200, 150, 100).

## c04 · Kodak built the future and kept the past

- In 1975 Steve Sasson, an engineer at Kodak, built the first self-contained digital camera (a prototype).  
  *Check:* National Inventors Hall of Fame, entry for Steven J. Sasson (digital camera); Kodak’s own company history; Sasson’s accounts in press interviews.
- The prototype recorded images of about 0.01 megapixels to a cassette tape, taking about 23 seconds per image.  
  *Check:* Same sources as above.
- In January 2012, Eastman Kodak filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the United States.  
  *Check:* Eastman Kodak press release, 19 January 2012; news reports of the same day (e.g. Reuters, BBC News).
- 37 years between the prototype and the filing.  
  *Check:* Arithmetic: 2012 − 1975 = 37.

## c05 · The first flight lasted about 12 seconds

- On 17 December 1903, at Kitty Hawk, North Carolina, Orville Wright made the first powered, controlled flight: about 12 seconds and about 120 feet.  
  *Check:* Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum, “1903 Wright Flyer”; US National Park Service, Wright Brothers National Memorial. (The site is Kill Devil Hills, near Kitty Hawk; the flight is usually described as at Kitty Hawk.)
- In 1901 the Wright brothers built a small wind tunnel and tested about 200 wing shapes in it.  
  *Check:* Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum, “The Wright Brothers: The Invention of the Aerial Age” (wind tunnel section); NASA Glenn Research Center pages on the Wrights’ 1901 wind tunnel.
- Two years between the wind tunnel tests and the first powered flight.  
  *Check:* Arithmetic: 1903 − 1901 = 2.
