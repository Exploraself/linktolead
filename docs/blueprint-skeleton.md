## Read this first

- **The spine stays.** Four domains, four moves (Tap · Slide · Write once · Four lines back), twelve words, three chapters, a reading after Levels 05, 09 and 12, a promise stated as things.
- **What is new is the end of each domain.** Every action now has a **day** (29 domains get a day screen) and a **Done when** you can check on Sunday, followed by one slide: how sure are you?
- **Inform, reform, transform.** Each domain now opens with a short insight (why it matters), ends in one action with a day (reform), and gives the founder something to keep, filled with their own words, that goes into their Blueprint (transform).
- **A true blueprint at week 12.** Everything the founder writes becomes one document in eight parts: leader, people, what you sell, marketing, sales, money, systems, the next twelve weeks.
- **One question per screen.** Each blank of the sentence gets its own screen. Days are taps. Enter moves on.
- **Every Sunday counts.** From Level 02, a level opens by asking about last week’s four actions: Done, Partly, Not yet. The readings show what was done and how good the estimates were.
- **Twenty actions a level, at the founder’s size.** The four from the founder’s own sentences are the **Do** actions. My Blueprint → This week adds, in each area, **Look** (open and count), **Decide** (write the line), **Involve** (one person or customer) and **Keep** (make it weekly): twenty in all, written for four sizes (before the first sale · selling alone · a team with payroll · 16 people or more). Every level, 01–12.
- **Feedback at every step** (Inform → Reform → Transform, never a label on screen). Every area opens on its idea, in one sentence; each tap gets a reply; the slider says what the number costs; each blank that needs it gets a check as it is written. All twelve levels.
- **Nothing is asked twice.** Where the Launchpad plan or the Growth Plan already covers a step, the level shows that answer back or links to it. Seven Launchpad actions that repeat a level become counts (proposal).
- **Decisions:** D1 to D7 are applied (1 Nov); D8, the book titles, is still yours. Saved answers keep their meaning: only new keys are added, so no migration.

#### S · Lead yourself · Settled

**Topic:** The rule that decides your worth

**Inform. The idea** (its own screen, before the tap): Somewhere you learned a rule for being worth something. It still runs your week, until you test it.

**Why it matters** (folded under the idea): A rule like this was learned early, often from someone you wanted to please. It still decides how you rest, price and say no. You do not argue a rule away; you test it once and look at what really happens.

1. **Tap.** Finish the sentence that runs your work: “If I ___, then I’m worth something.”  
   Options, each with its reply (Reform: why it doesn’t hold, or that it works):
   - 1 *hit my numbers*: Numbers move with markets, seasons and luck. If your worth moves with them, every bad month becomes a verdict on you.
   - 2 *work harder than everyone*: Someone will always work longer. This rule has no finish line, so it never lets you rest.
   - 3 *never let anyone down*: Someone is always let down: a client, your family, or you. The rule only chooses who.
   - 4 *prove them wrong*: Then they still decide your week, even when they are not in the room.
   - 5 *get it perfect*: Perfect never ships. The rule keeps the work in your hands and off the market.
2. **Slide.** Last month, how often did that rule decide your day?  
   Scale 0 to 10, step 1; ends “Never” and “Every day”; starts at 5. Words: up to 2: rarely · 5: some days · 8: most days · 10: every day.
   What it costs, as it moves: at 0, “Never: the rule is already quiet. Test it once anyway, on purpose.” · at 5, “The rule chose for you some days. Those are days you did not choose.” · at 10, “The rule chose for you every day. Those are days you did not choose.”
3. **Write once**, one blank per screen, the sentence building above it:  
   “I am ___ before I achieve anything. On ___ I will ___.”
   - `who`: worth it, enough… (e.g. *enough*) · check: ✓ Good. That is who you are before any result.
   - `day`: which day (e.g. *Thursday*) · day chips
   - `act`: one thing the old rule forbids (e.g. *take the evening off, phone in a drawer*) · check (at least 3 words): ✓ Good: on {day} anyone could see you do it. / → Make it something someone could see you do. A few more words.
4. **Done when** (shown, not typed): on *Thursday* you did “*take the evening off, phone in a drawer*”, and wrote one line on what really happened.  
   Then one slide: “How sure are you that this is done by Sunday?” 0–100%.
5. **Four lines back** (the Version 4 example founder):
   - *Where you were. Admit it.* Last month the rule If I *hit my numbers*, then I’m worth something ran your day *most days*.
   - *Where you struggle. Don’t fight it.* It was learned, so it can be tested. Don’t argue with it. Test it once, on purpose.
   - *Where you shine. Don’t ignore it.* You named your rule in one sentence. That is the first lever, and you pulled it.
   - *What you will do. Exactly.* On *Thursday* you will *take the evening off, phone in a drawer*, and write what really happened. You are *enough* before you achieve anything.

**Transform. Yours to keep** (under the four lines, with a Copy button; it goes into the Blueprint part *You, as a leader*): **The test card**
- Old rule: If I *hit my numbers*, then I’m worth something.
- The test: on *Thursday* I *take the evening off, phone in a drawer*.
- What I feared would happen: ____
- What really happened: ____

#### E · Lead your people · Entrust

**Topic:** One task out of your hands

**Inform. The idea** (its own screen, before the tap): Trust is built in small, checked steps. A clear “done” protects both of you.

**Why it matters** (folded under the idea): Every task you keep because “nobody does it right” costs you an hour you needed for the work only you can do. Trust is built in small, checked steps, not in one leap. A clear “done” protects both of you.

1. **Tap.** Finish it honestly: “People usually ___.”  
   Options, each with its reply (Reform: why it doesn’t hold, or that it works):
   - 1 *let me down*: Expecting it, you hand over half a task and check every hour. Nobody can rise to a task they were never fully given.
   - 2 *do it worse than I would*: At first, maybe. But your way can be written down. A task you never hand over can never get better without you.
   - 3 *need me to check everything*: Checking everything makes you the bottleneck and leaves them unsure. One agreed “done”, checked once, does more.
   - 4 *rise when they are trusted*: That belief works. Now give it one real task this week.
2. **Slide.** How much of last week’s work did you hold because “nobody else can do it right”?  
   Scale 0% to 100%, step 10; ends “None” and “All of it”; starts at 50. Words: 0% reads “some” · 50% reads “a lot” · 100% reads “almost all of it”.
   What it costs, as it moves: at 0%, “None. Your week is already shared; keep it that way.” · at 50%, “50% of last week stayed with you. Every hour of it came out of the work only you can do.” · at 100%, “100% of last week stayed with you. Every hour of it came out of the work only you can do.”
3. **Write once**, one blank per screen, the sentence building above it:  
   “I hand ___ to ___ on ___. Done means ___.”
   - `task`: one task (e.g. *the weekly invoices*) · check (at least 2 words): ✓ One task, named. / → Name the task so they would know exactly what it is.
   - `who`: a name (e.g. *Jordan*) · check (not a vague word): ✓ Good: a real name. / → A real name, so it can happen on the day.
   - `day`: which day (e.g. *Monday*) · day chips
   - `done`: what finished looks like (e.g. *all sent by 5 pm*) · check (at least 3 words): ✓ Good: they will know when it is finished, and you check once. / → Say what finished looks like, so you only check once.
4. **Done when** (shown, not typed): on *Monday* you handed *the weekly invoices* to *Jordan*, with “*all sent by 5 pm*” in writing, and checked it only once.  
   Then one slide: “How sure are you that this is done by Sunday?” 0–100%.
5. **Four lines back** (the Version 4 example founder):
   - *Where you were. Admit it.* About *60%* of last week, you held work because nobody else could do it right.
   - *Where you struggle. Don’t fight it.* You believe people usually *do it worse than I would*. Don’t fight the feeling. Test it in one small step.
   - *Where you shine. Don’t ignore it.* You named one task to let go. That is where trust starts.
   - *What you will do. Exactly.* On *Monday* you hand *the weekly invoices* to *Jordan*. Done means *all sent by 5 pm*. You check once, not every hour.

- Done means: *all sent by 5 pm*.
