SLead yourself · Settled

The rule that decides your worth

Inform · the idea, before the tap Somewhere you learned a rule for being worth something. It still runs your week, until you test it.

Why it matters (folded under it) A rule like this was learned early, often from someone you wanted to please. It still decides how you rest, price and say no. You do not argue a rule away; you test it once and look at what really happens.

  1. Tap. Finish the sentence that runs your work: “If I ___, then I’m worth something.”
    • 1hit my numbers → Numbers move with markets, seasons and luck. If your worth moves with them, every bad month becomes a verdict on you.
    • 2work harder than everyone → Someone will always work longer. This rule has no finish line, so it never lets you rest.
    • 3never let anyone down → Someone is always let down: a client, your family, or you. The rule only chooses who.
    • 4prove them wrong → Then they still decide your week, even when they are not in the room.
    • 5get it perfect → Perfect never ships. The rule keeps the work in your hands and off the market.
  2. Slide. Last month, how often did that rule decide your day?

    Scale 0 to 10, step 1 · ends “Never” and “Every day” · starts at 5
    up to 2: rarely · 5: some days · 8: most days · 10: every day

    • 0 Never: the rule is already quiet. Test it once anyway, on purpose.
    • 5 The rule chose for you some days. Those are days you did not choose.
    • 10 The rule chose for you every day. Those are days you did not choose.
  3. Write once, one blank per screen

    “I am ___ before I achieve anything. On ___ I will ___.”

    • who worth it, enough… e.g. enough
      ✓ Good. That is who you are before any result.
    • day which day e.g. Thursdayday chips
    • act one thing the old rule forbids e.g. take the evening off, phone in a drawer
      ✓ Good: on {day} anyone could see you do it. · → Make it something someone could see you do. A few more words.
  4. Done when new on Thursday you did “take the evening off, phone in a drawer”, and wrote one line on what really happened.

    Then: “How sure are you that this is done by Sunday?” 0–100%.

  5. Four lines back
    Where you were Admit it.
    Last month the rule If I hit my numbers, then I’m worth something ran your day most days.
    Where you struggle Don’t fight it.
    It was learned, so it can be tested. Don’t argue with it. Test it once, on purpose.
    Where you shine Don’t ignore it.
    You named your rule in one sentence. That is the first lever, and you pulled it.
    What you will do Exactly.
    On Thursday you will take the evening off, phone in a drawer, and write what really happened. You are enough before you achieve anything.

One task out of your hands

Inform · the idea, before the tap Trust is built in small, checked steps. A clear “done” protects both of you.

Why it matters (folded under it) Every task you keep because “nobody does it right” costs you an hour you needed for the work only you can do. Trust is built in small, checked steps, not in one leap. A clear “done” protects both of you.

  1. Tap. Finish it honestly: “People usually ___.”

    • 2do it worse than I would → At first, maybe. But your way can be written down. A task you never hand over can never get better without you.
    • 3need me to check everything → Checking everything makes you the bottleneck and leaves them unsure. One agreed “done”, checked once, does more.
    • 4rise when they are trusted → That belief works. Now give it one real task this week.

ELead your business · Enough

Money: the sentence you grew up with, and your enough

Inform · the idea, before the tap Enough is a finish line, not a ceiling. Without a number, every month feels short.

Why it matters (folded under it) Without a number for enough, every month feels short and every price feels too high. Enough is a finish line, not a ceiling: it tells you when to stop chasing and what to charge. The sentence you grew up with is not your business plan.

  1. Tap. Which money sentence did you hear most growing up?
    • 1There is never enough → Then no amount will ever feel like enough, because you never set one. A number ends the chase.
    • 2Money is for greedy people → Then charging fairly feels wrong, and your prices stay low. Fair pay is how the business keeps serving.
    • 3We don’t talk about money → Silence keeps the numbers vague, and vague numbers turn every decision into a guess.
    • 4Money proves you are someone → Then every price is a test of your worth. Money is a tool; your worth is not on the invoice.
    • 5Work hard and it will come → Hard work with no number can run forever. Enough tells you when the work has done its job.
  2. Slide. Money in, fixed costs, what was left last month: how sure are you of all three?

    Scale 0 to 10, step 1 · ends “Guessing” and “Exactly” · starts at 4
    up to 3: mostly guessing · 6: roughly · 8: quite sure · 10: certain

    • 0 You are mostly guessing about the three numbers. From a guess, you cannot tell when you have enough.
    • 4 You are roughly about the three numbers. From a guess, you cannot tell when you have enough.
    • 10 You know your numbers. Now give them a finish line.
  3. Write once, one blank per screen

    “My enough is ___ a month. On ___ I will ___.”

    • enough an amount e.g. $4,200
      ✓ A number. Now money has a finish line. · → Enough is a number: write an amount, like $4,200. · A spreadsheet adds money in, costs and what was left in seconds. Deciding what is enough is yours.
    • day which day e.g. Wednesdayday chips
